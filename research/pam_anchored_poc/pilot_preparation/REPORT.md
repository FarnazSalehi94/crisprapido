# HG00735_pat targeted follow-up and pilot preparation

2026-11-06. All **13** previously untested repetitive exact-pattern cases were located and checked under a 20,000-entry suffix-array cap, with no skips. The final contig was reached on both strands, including a reverse-strand 23-mer ending 343 bases before the end of the concatenated reference. A natural reverse-strand target containing two reference Ns was recovered with two mismatches; natural PAMs containing Ns in required G positions were rejected on both strands. These are targeted checks, not a genome-wide sensitivity measurement. No whole-assembly guide search or Slurm job was launched.

## Repetitive intervals and coordinates

The earlier 64-entry cap bounded expansion of an exact 23-mer's suffix-array interval; it did not indicate failure or an incorrect coordinate. Repeats have multiple indexed occurrences, and the original 13 skipped interval widths were **66 to 18,756**. The follow-up builds a separate research probe from the unchanged prototype and permits at most 20,000 suffix-array entries per case. It expands the exact interval, checks that `findSA` includes the specified global position, and uses the prototype's real emission logic to check the contig, strand, PAM anchor, true reference and mismatch count. The method is resource bounded but still enumerates candidates within an interval; an interval wider than 20,000 would remain a reported skip rather than a negative result. The 20,000 cap is specific to this targeted probe, not a production search limit.

All 13 formerly skipped cases and four natural-N cases passed; there were **17 completed, zero skipped**, with pre- and post-use reference/index binding checks. An independent `samtools faidx` reader retrieved all 17 original compressed-source windows and compared them with the imported FASTA and `.txt.bin`. The code checks global offsets against the pinned FAI and `.pos`. See [cases](results/cases.json), [source comparisons](results/independent.json), and [case-level outcomes](results/probe-stdout.log). The previous 64- and 128-entry runs independently covered additional loci on both sides of 2³¹ and both sides of a contig junction, including two correctly rejected cross-contig patterns; see the [previous report](../large_coordinate/REPORT.md). No general conclusion about approximate search traversal follows from these exact lookups.

## Natural and synthetic N checks

A natural reverse-strand target at global start 1,384,048,353, contig `HG00735#1#JAHBCH020000003.1`, local start 216,971 and anchor 216,974, has oriented reference `NNTTATCCACTTCTGTCCCAAGG`; its indexed substitution starts `CC`. The indexed pattern located the expected suffix and true-reference replay produced **two mismatches** with strict `AGG`. The plus-strand natural target from the previous report was reconfirmed with three mismatches. These exact indexed words are constructed using the substituted text; this demonstrates replay and locating, not that approximate DFS will always reach every N-containing hit.

Natural plus and minus cases had true PAMs `ANN` and `CNN` respectively while the indexed substitutions were `AGG` and `CGG`. Both targets had zero guide mismatches, and `Search::emit` rejected both, independently of the mismatch limit. No eligible natural example with N specifically at the wildcard PAM position was selected by the bounded N-run discovery; this remains a natural-data gap. The prior in-memory controls reject that case on both strands.

A bounded scan of the first and last 1,000 bases of each of the 82 imported contigs found **no Ns** in those windows ([boundary-n.json](results/boundary-n.json)); therefore it cannot provide a natural boundary-N example. Instead, the existing independent exhaustive [reference-N fixture](../validation/test_reference_n.py) was rerun: its targets touch contig boundaries, cover both strands, aligned Ns, Ns near PAM and gaps, consecutive Ns and N in each PAM position. It recovered **12/12 valid anchors**, with zero unexpected or duplicate anchors, and rejected all six invalid PAM cases. This small synthetic fixture does not replace a natural large-coordinate boundary-N test.

## Resources and batch reuse

This single follow-up run's sampled aggregate process-group RSS peaked at **14,226,448 KiB (13.57 GiB)**. Its native index constructor took 3.86 s, full native reference loading 18.71 s and the 17 bounded exact lookups 0.061 s; wall time including the two full binding verifications was 359.63 s. Sampling at 100 ms can miss peaks and summed RSS may double-count shared pages. These results omit the integrated CLI's snapshot, Python reference copies, buffered search output, scoring and full approximate search; they are **not** a whole-guide RAM or runtime estimate. See [measurements](results/measurements.json).

Source inspection confirms the integrated `-G` path sends all guides to one `run_snapshot.py` invocation, which creates one snapshot and invokes the native search once; the native process loads the index once before looping over guides. There is no per-guide snapshot within a batch. Repeated CLI invocations still each copy and verify the bundle. No batch-loading redesign was made.

For a strictly bounded resource pilot, the proposed *starting limits*, not demonstrated requirements, are **one CPU, 64 GiB cgroup memory, two hours wall time, at least 60 GB initially free scratch beyond the persistent 14.54 GB bundle, and a 10 GiB per-file output limit**. Search state, Python output buffers, hit count, audit time and filesystem load could exceed these caps. An external scratch quota and monitoring are still required: a free-space precheck and a per-file limit do not cap aggregate scratch consumption. `/tmp` had only about 36 GiB free at inspection; `/export` reported substantially more, but a future worker must check its own mount, quota and sharing conditions. CPU/wall limits are caps, not predicted completion times. If any limit fires, the result is incomplete; absence of a site from an incomplete output says nothing about recall.

## Prepared Slurm pilot

[one_guide.slurm](one_guide.slurm) is a **preparation artifact and has not been submitted or exercised under Slurm**. It requests one task/CPU, 64 GiB and two hours; it fails closed unless a numeric enforced cgroup-v2 `memory.max` of at most 64 GiB is visible, the selected scratch has 60 GB free, and expected bundle manifest, FAI and explicitly supplied executable SHA256 values match. It uses the imported bundle and existing snapshot path; it runs exactly one 20-base `GUIDE` with explicit `-m 5 -b 1 -z 2 -f 0.75 -p GG -t 1`. The operator must supply absolute `REPO`, `CRISPR_BIN`, `PAM_BIN`, `RESULT_ROOT`, `SCRATCH_ROOT`, pinned `CRISPR_SHA256` and `PAM_SHA256`, and `GUIDE`. For reproducibility a possible test input is `TGCCTGGAATAAATCTCACT` from the existing independent panel; its HG00735 repeat burden and whole-assembly runtime are unmeasured. The CLI/native binaries and their embedded helper/interpreter dependencies must be freshly built or verified for the execution node, without modifying production binaries.

The script records progress, cgroup memory and free disk, `/usr/bin/time -v`, verifies the complete bundle before and after the workflow, and separately hashes the exact reference after auditing. The experimental adapter also performs its own snapshot/binding checks. Results remain named `.incomplete` and status remains `INCOMPLETE` until the workflow, postcheck and independent PAF audit all succeed. The audit checks reference bases, bounds, strands, strict PAM, CIGAR replay, mismatch and bulge limits, match fraction, duplicate anchors and independently computed CFD where applicable. It is **an eligibility audit of emitted rows, not a completeness oracle**. The script uses an unsigned, SHA-pinned binding and regular filesystem paths; it does not eliminate a malicious same-user mutate-and-restore race. Use a trusted, isolated execution environment. Slurm cgroup-v1 sites, different mount paths, missing helper dependencies or insufficient scratch fail rather than silently weakening checks. The scheduler's hard wall/memory enforcement, storage quota and signal behavior need site-specific verification before a real job; `bash -n` and auditor tests do not establish cluster behavior.

## Readiness and remaining work

The new targeted evidence closes the 13 capped cases, adds final-contig lookups on both strands, natural reverse-strand N replay and natural required-G N rejection. It does **not** establish natural wildcard-PAM N or natural boundary-N cases, every near-end position, large-coordinate approximate mismatch/bulge recall, whole-assembly sensitivity, or complete-CLI peak memory and throughput. The prototype's 32-bit index format and specific index variant remain constraints; this assembly fits but other HPRC assemblies may not. A controlled **resource pilot** is reasonable only after site-specific review of Slurm/cgroup enforcement, storage quota, executable and helper provenance, and the incomplete-output handling described above. Its outcome would measure resources and emitted-site eligibility for one guide, not establish genome-wide completeness or clinical validity. No pilot is authorized or launched by this report.
